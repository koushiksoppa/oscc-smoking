# Smoked tobacco use and the risk of squamous cell carcinoma of the oral cavity: protocol and interim screening report for a systematic review and meta-analysis

**Manuscript status: DRAFT — not a results-bearing paper.** Sections 7.2 to 7.5, 8 and 9 cannot be
completed because no study has yet been assessed for eligibility at full text. Those sections are
present with their outstanding requirements stated explicitly. No result is reported that the
underlying data do not support.

---

## 2. Abstract

**Background.** Smoked tobacco is an established carcinogen, and oral cavity cancer carries high
morbidity and a poor prognosis in late-stage presentation. Quantitative estimates of the
association between smoked tobacco and squamous cell carcinoma arising specifically in the oral
cavity are dispersed across case–control and cohort literature that frequently reports pooled
head-and-neck outcomes, which obscures site-specific effect sizes.

**Objective.** To determine whether smoked tobacco use is associated with an increased risk of
squamous cell carcinoma of the oral cavity.

**Methods.** A protocol was specified in advance, defining the outcome as oral cavity cancer with
squamous cell carcinoma histologically confirmed or a stated majority of at least 90%, and the
exposure as any combusted tobacco product. Eligible designs are case–control and cohort studies
reporting an odds ratio, risk ratio or hazard ratio with a 95% confidence interval, or sufficient
raw data to compute one. One database, PubMed, has been searched to date. Records are screened by
a single reviewer against a controlled vocabulary of exclusion reasons, with every decision
recorded alongside the basis on which it was made.

**Results.** The executed search retrieved 194 records, of which 194 were unique reports after
deduplication (0 duplicates). A provisional screening pass classified 109 reports for exclusion
and 85 for retrieval. **These classifications were produced by an automated procedure and adopted
without individual human adjudication; under the review's own protocol they do not constitute
completed screening, and the formal PRISMA flow therefore reports 0 reports screened and 0 taken
forward.** No full text has been sought, retrieved or assessed. No study has been included, no
data extracted, no risk of bias appraised and no estimate pooled.

**Conclusions.** *Not yet available.* No conclusion about the association between smoked tobacco
and oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma can be drawn from this work in its current state. This
report documents the protocol, the search executed to date and the interim screening position.

**Registration.** Not registered. Prospective registration remains an open decision, and the
provisional screening pass was performed without it; this is reported as a protocol deviation.

## 4. Introduction

Cancer of the oral cavity is among the more common malignancies worldwide, with a markedly uneven
geographical distribution and a substantial burden in South and Southeast Asia. The great majority
of oral cavity malignancies are squamous cell carcinomas arising from the mucosal lining. Survival
is strongly stage-dependent, and a large proportion of cases present late, so primary prevention —
and therefore accurate characterisation of modifiable risk factors — carries disproportionate
weight.

Tobacco smoking is an established carcinogen for the upper aerodigestive tract. Quantifying its
association with oral cavity cancer specifically is, however, complicated by three recurring
features of the primary literature. First, many studies report outcomes pooled across the head and
neck, combining oral cavity, oropharyngeal, hypopharyngeal and laryngeal sites whose aetiologies
differ, notably in the contribution of human papillomavirus to oropharyngeal disease. Second, the
exposure is frequently reduced to cigarette smoking, omitting bidis, cigars, pipes and other
combusted products that dominate exposure in some of the highest-incidence populations. Third,
alcohol consumption, smokeless tobacco and betel quid are strongly correlated with smoking in many
settings and are variably adjusted for, so estimates are not directly comparable across studies.

A review restricted to oral-cavity-specific outcomes, admitting the full range of combusted tobacco
products and recording confounder adjustment explicitly for each estimate, is therefore warranted.

## 6. Methods

This review is designed and reported in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 statement [1], with
search reporting following the PRISMA-S extension [2] and the conduct of a review of observational
studies following the MOOSE recommendations [3]. Methodological conventions not otherwise
attributed follow the Cochrane Handbook [4].

### 6.1 Eligibility criteria

**Inclusion.**

1. Observational design: case–control or cohort (prospective or retrospective).
2. Reports an odds ratio, risk ratio or hazard ratio with a 95% confidence interval, **or**
   sufficient raw data to calculate one.
3. Outcome meets the definition in §5: oral cavity cancer, squamous cell carcinoma histologically
   confirmed or a stated majority of at least 90%, **with an oral-cavity-specific estimate**.
   Estimates pooled across head-and-neck subsites are not eligible unless an oral-cavity-specific
   estimate is reported separately.
4. Exposure is any smoked tobacco, with a defined reference group.
5. Human participants.
6. Published in English.

**Exclusion** is recorded against a fixed controlled vocabulary; free text may not substitute for a
code. The codes available at title and abstract are `wrong_population`, `wrong_outcome`,
`wrong_exposure`, `wrong_design`, `review_or_meta`, `conference_abstract`, `animal_or_invitro`,
`not_english` and `other` (the last requiring a written justification). Four further codes —
`no_reference_group`, `no_usable_data`, `outcome_not_separable` and `duplicate_publication` — are
restricted to full text, on the grounds that they cannot be judged from an abstract without
guessing. **The stage assignment of these codes remains a proposal awaiting formal endorsement.**

Smokeless tobacco and betel quid are treated as **confounders, not exposures**, and adjustment for
each is recorded per estimate.

**Criteria not yet settled.** Nine questions concerning anatomical and histological scope remain
open: the eligible subsite list; the treatment of soft palate and uvula, where staging and
anatomical conventions genuinely conflict; the handling of straddling topography codes; a
site-purity threshold analogous to the ≥90% histology threshold; whether squamous cell carcinoma
variants count as squamous cell carcinoma; and the status of adenosquamous and sarcomatoid
entities. Two of these require a reference text that was not accessible during protocol
development. A tenth question — whether populations restricted to premalignant oral lesions are in
scope — affects 14 of the 85 reports provisionally taken forward and has not yet been assigned an
identifier. **These must be resolved before full-text eligibility assessment begins.**

### 6.2 Information sources and search strategy

One bibliographic database, **PubMed**, has been searched. The protocol acknowledges that a single
database does not constitute a complete systematic search; which additional sources to search is an
open decision (see §8.3).

Three search executions are distinguished in the project record, because a database's content and
the filters actually applied may differ between runs:

| Execution | Query | Filters applied | Records | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 (original) | see below | Humans, English | 138 | **Export lost.** Not recreated or substituted; contributes no records |
| v1r (re-execution) | identical to v1 | **none** | **194** | Registered with checksum; the evidence base for all work reported here |
| v2 (expanded) | broader MeSH and free-text strategy | none | 4,770 | **Drafted and syntax-validated; not executed** |

The strategy, the filters actually applied, the platform and the counts are reported per
execution in line with PRISMA-S [2]. Query string, identical for v1 and v1r:

```
("Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma"[Title/Abstract] OR OSCC[Title/Abstract])
AND
(smoking[Title/Abstract] OR cigarette[Title/Abstract])
AND
("case-control"[Title/Abstract] OR cohort[Title/Abstract])
```

**Deviation from the v1 strategy, reported as such.** The re-execution was run *without* the Humans
and English database filters. The Humans filter operates on an index term that is absent from
records not yet indexed — 42 of the 194 here, which are the most recent and therefore plausibly
among the most relevant. Non-English and non-human records are instead excluded at screening with a
recorded reason code, which is auditable in a way a database filter is not. The consequence, which
must be reported, is that `not_english` and `wrong_population` appear as exclusion reasons that a
filtered search would never have surfaced.

**Search date.** The execution date of the v1r search is **not recorded**. Index dates within the
export bound it to on or after 25 October 2026. The execution date of the original v1 search is
**unrecoverable** from any surviving file. Both are reported as unverified rather than estimated.

**Reconciliation.** Re-running the v1 query string on 29 October 2026 returned 194 records
unfiltered and 150 with the Humans and English filters applied, against the 138 recorded in the v1
strategy document. Three records had entered the database since the project began. A residual
discrepancy of approximately nine records between the documented 138 and the ~147 implied remains
**unexplained and is recorded as an open question**. The documented count has not been altered to
remove the discrepancy.

**Limitations of the executed query, stated plainly.** The v1 string is narrow relative to the
eligibility criteria. It requires the literal phrases "Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma" or "OSCC" in
the title or abstract, which omits the large body of aetiological literature reporting "oral
cancer" or "oral cavity cancer"; it reduces the exposure to "smoking" or "cigarette", omitting
bidi, cigar, pipe and other product terms central to the exposure definition; and it requires a
literal design term. The expanded v2 strategy was drafted to address this and retrieves 4,770
records, a volume whose feasibility under single-reviewer screening is unresolved. **Any synthesis
based on the 194-record set alone would rest on a search that does not match its own protocol, and
this must be stated as a principal limitation.**

### 6.3 Study selection

Screening is performed by **one reviewer**. This is a resource constraint, declared openly, and is
reported as a limitation under PRISMA 2020 items 8 and 9 [1]. There is no second screener and no
duplicate screening at any stage.

Records are deduplicated before screening by identifier and by an exact author–year–title
fingerprint. Records whose titles are merely *similar* are flagged for human review and are never
merged automatically, because deciding that two records describe the same report is a judgement.

**Automated classification and its status.** An automated procedure generated a suggested decision
and, where applicable, a suggested exclusion reason for each record from its title and abstract.
That suggestion set is retained as an audit artefact. Each worksheet row records both the
suggestion and the basis on which the recorded decision was made, using two values:
`adopted_from_suggestion`, meaning the suggestion was accepted without the record being judged
individually; and `independently_adjudicated`, meaning the reviewer judged the record from its own
evidence.

**An automated suggestion cannot satisfy a decision field.** A stage containing any
`adopted_from_suggestion` row is not a screened stage, however complete it appears. The reporting
consequence is set out in §7.1: the formal PRISMA flow counts independently adjudicated decisions
only, and adopted classifications are reported separately as a provisional outcome.

A decision counts as independently adjudicated only when all five of the following are recorded: a
decision, a reviewer, a screening date, the basis `independently_adjudicated`, and a separate
adjudication date.

**Blinded verification.** Because the recorded classifications are identical to the automated
suggestions, a check of those classifications against the same source would be circular. A blinded
verification sample of 40 of the 194 records (20.6%) was drawn by simple random sampling without
replacement, with the recorded decision withheld, using a seed derived from the data rather than
chosen. Acceptance thresholds, the sample size and a one-directional sweep rule were all fixed
before the sample was drawn and before any response existed. Agreement is computed at the record
level on the decision value alone, and Cohen's kappa [6] is reported alongside it as a descriptive
statistic rather than as an acceptance criterion. **The methodology governing this exercise is specified and
implemented but has not yet been formally adopted as a protocol decision**; a decision entry is
drafted and awaiting approval.

This exercise compares **one reviewer's blinded decisions against an automated suggestion set**. It
is not inter-reviewer agreement and not independent duplicate screening. A favourable result would
not retrospectively convert suggestion-adopted records into independently adjudicated ones.

### 6.4 Data extraction

Extraction is performed by one reviewer into a fixed schema of 34 study-level and 47 estimate-level
fields. Three principles are enforced by validation rather than left to discipline: reported and
calculated values never share a field; missing is a value, recorded explicitly as *not reported*,
*not applicable* or *unclear*, and never as blank, zero or inferred; and every extracted number
carries a source location naming the page, table or figure it came from.

Recorded per estimate: the effect measure and its confidence interval as reported; the covariate
list verbatim; separate indicator fields for adjustment for alcohol, age, sex, socioeconomic
status, smokeless tobacco, betel quid and human papillomavirus; exposure and comparator definitions
verbatim; product types; and smoking intensity, duration, pack-years and any other cumulative
metric, each with its own field and unit so that a study reporting several is not reduced to one.
Recorded per study: design, setting, recruitment period, participant counts, histological
confirmation and the reported percentage of squamous cell carcinoma, anatomical subsites verbatim,
whether an oral-cavity-specific estimate is separable, case definition, the matching variables used
in design, and the method by which exposure was ascertained.

Two fields carry an explicit unresolved status in the schema and cannot be treated as
protocol-conformant until the corresponding questions are settled: the comparator type and the
exposure contrast.

**No data have been extracted.** The extraction dataset does not exist.

### 6.5 Outcomes and exposure definitions

**Outcome.** Cancer of the oral cavity in which squamous cell carcinoma is histologically
confirmed, or in which squamous cell carcinoma is a stated majority of at least 90% of cases. The
threshold exists because most aetiological literature reports "oral cancer" with a stated
histological composition rather than using the phrase "oral squamous cell carcinoma"; requiring the
literal phrase would select a non-representative subset. Estimates pooled across head-and-neck
subsites are ineligible unless an oral-cavity-specific estimate is reported separately.

**Exposure.** Any smoked tobacco — cigarettes, bidis, cigars, pipes and other combusted products.
Product type is extracted for every estimate to support a planned subgroup analysis of cigarette-
only versus mixed-product exposure. A cigarette-only review would exclude much of the most
informative literature, particularly South Asian studies of bidi and reverse smoking, where the
exposure burden is greatest.

**Confounders of primary concern.** Alcohol; smokeless tobacco; betel quid. These are exposures in
their own right for other questions but are confounders here, and adjustment for each is recorded
separately per estimate.

### 6.6 Risk-of-bias assessment

**No instrument has been selected, and no assessment has been performed.**

The appraisal table is specified instrument-agnostically — one row per study per domain, recording
the instrument and its version, the domain, the judgement on the instrument's own scale, supporting
evidence quoted or located in the paper, the reviewer's justification, whether information was
missing, and who assessed it. Domain names will come from the chosen instrument and are not
invented. No numeric summary score will be computed unless the chosen instrument explicitly defines
one.

Three candidates have been set out for decision: the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale [7]; ROBINS-E [8]; and
Newcastle–Ottawa reported per domain without a total score. The choice has a second component that
cannot be deferred: which confounders a study must address for the confounding or comparability
domain to be judged adequate — alcohol alone, or alcohol together with smokeless tobacco and betel
quid. An instrument that asks whether confounding was handled adequately is unanswerable until
"adequately" is defined, and defining it after seeing the studies would be post hoc. **This
decision is pending.**

Missing information will not be treated as evidence of high risk of bias.

### 6.7 Statistical analysis

The analysis implementation exists and is tested; **it has not been run, and it refuses to run**,
because no study has been included and no estimate extracted.

**Specified in advance.** Standard errors are derived as
`SE = (ln(upper) − ln(lower)) / (2 × 1.96)` where a 95% confidence interval is reported, and from
raw cell counts where only a 2 × 2 table is given; which route was used is recorded per estimate,
and reported and calculated values are held in separate fields. At most one estimate per study
enters any single analysis. Fundamentally different effect measures are not combined without a
documented justification, and no transformation is applied merely to make pooling easier.
Heterogeneity will be quantified by Cochran's Q and I², with τ² reported for random-effects models.

**Not yet specified, because it depends on evidence that does not exist.** The primary effect
measure, and whether case–control and cohort estimates may be pooled at all or must be analysed
separately, depend on which designs are included. The model and estimator — a random-effects
model is anticipated, with the DerSimonian–Laird [5] and restricted maximum likelihood estimators
both implemented — and whether a small-sample adjustment is applied, depend on the number of
studies. The exposure contrast and the
reference-group handling depend on the two open protocol questions in §5. The rule for choosing
among several adjusted models reported in one paper is recommended but not adopted.

**Planned subgroup analyses**, contingent on sufficient studies: product type (cigarette-only versus
mixed); geographical region; anatomical subsite; risk-of-bias level; and alcohol-adjusted versus
unadjusted estimates. **Planned sensitivity analyses** and assessment of small-study effects are
specified in the analysis plan and are likewise contingent.

**Declared implementation limitations.** The restricted maximum likelihood estimator has no
external validation in this implementation; it is tested for self-consistency and limiting
behaviour only, and any estimate produced with it must be cross-checked against an established
implementation before publication. No Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman interval is implemented, which
the Cochrane Handbook [4] favours when the number of studies is small and between-study variance is
non-zero — the situation anticipated here. No Mantel–Haenszel or Peto method is implemented, so
rare-event and zero-cell data have no route. No meta-regression or dose–response model is
implemented. The pooled estimate, weights and τ² have no external reference check.

## 8. Discussion

### 8.1 Principal findings

No finding about the association between smoked tobacco use and squamous cell carcinoma of the oral
cavity is available from this work. What can be stated is the position of the review itself: a
protocol with most eligibility criteria settled and several of scope still open; one database
searched with a query narrower than the protocol requires; 194 unique reports identified; and a
provisional screening pass that has not been independently adjudicated and therefore contributes no
PRISMA-countable outcome.

### 8.2 Interpretation

The gap between the 194 records retrieved and the 4,770 the expanded strategy returns is the single
most consequential feature of the present position. It indicates that the executed search samples a
small and probably unrepresentative fraction of the eligible literature — specifically, that part
of it which happens to use the phrase "oral squamous cell carcinoma" or "OSCC" together with
"smoking" or "cigarette". Studies reporting "oral cancer" with stated histology, or exposure to
bidis or other combusted products, are systematically less likely to be retrieved. Any estimate
derived from the 194-record set would therefore be at material risk of selection bias of unknown
direction, and the decision on search scope should be taken before, not after, screening effort is
invested.

The distinction maintained throughout between a provisional classification and an adjudicated
decision is not a formality. All 194 recorded classifications are byte-identical to the automated
suggestions, with no per-record human approval. Reporting them as completed screening would
misstate what has been done, and would also misstate what the blinded verification exercise can
establish: it compares one reviewer's blinded decisions against an automated suggestion set, and
even a high agreement rate would not make the unadjudicated records adjudicated.

### 8.3 Strengths and limitations

**Strengths.** The protocol fixes the outcome and exposure definitions, the exclusion vocabulary
and the analysis conventions in advance, with each traceable to a dated decision record. The raw
export is immutable and checksum-verified, and the derived datasets rebuild byte-for-byte from it.
The provenance of every screening classification is recorded in the data rather than in prose, and
the reporting machinery refuses to count an unadjudicated classification as a screening outcome.
The blinded verification instrument, its sample size, its seed derivation and its acceptance
thresholds were all fixed before any response was recorded.

**Limitations.**

1. **Search coverage.** One database has been searched. PubMed alone does not constitute a
   systematic search, and no decision has been taken on which further sources to include.
2. **Search sensitivity.** The executed query is narrower than the protocol's own eligibility
   criteria, for the reasons given in §6.2. This is the dominant threat to validity at present.
3. **Lost original export.** The v1 export does not survive and has not been recreated. A residual
   discrepancy of about nine records in the reconciliation is unexplained.
4. **Unrecorded search dates.** Neither the v1 nor the v1r execution date is recorded; both are
   reported as unverified.
5. **Single reviewer.** No duplicate screening, extraction or appraisal at any stage.
6. **Screening not adjudicated.** All 194 classifications rest on an adopted automated suggestion.
7. **Verification incomplete.** The blinded sample is unanswered; no agreement figure exists.
8. **Scope questions open.** Ten questions of anatomical and histological scope, including the
   status of premalignant-lesion populations, remain unresolved.
9. **No registration.** The review is not prospectively registered, and the provisional screening
   pass preceded any registration decision.
10. **Analysis implementation.** The limitations declared in §6.7 apply to any future synthesis.

### 8.4 Implications

**For practice and policy.** None. No evidence is presented here that could support a
recommendation.

**For research conduct.** The project demonstrates a reporting arrangement in which an automated
classification cannot be mistaken for a human screening decision: the two are distinguished in the
data, enforced at validation, and reported in separate sections of the flow. Where automated
assistance is used in screening, this separation is what allows a reader to see what was actually
adjudicated.

**For completion of this review.** The outstanding work is set out in §9 and in the accompanying
project record. It is substantial and is human work.

## 9. Conclusion

**No conclusion regarding the association between smoked tobacco use and squamous cell carcinoma of
the oral cavity can be drawn from this work at present.**

A protocol has been specified and a single database searched, yielding 194 unique reports. A
provisional screening pass exists but has not been independently adjudicated and therefore
contributes nothing to the formal reporting flow. No full text has been retrieved, no study
included, no datum extracted, no risk of bias appraised and no estimate pooled. The search as
executed is narrower than the protocol requires and covers one database.

Completion requires, in order: a decision on search coverage and on the volume implied by the
expanded strategy; independent adjudication of the 194 classifications and completion of the
blinded verification sample; resolution of the open scope, comparator and contrast questions and of
the risk-of-bias instrument; retrieval and eligibility assessment of the reports taken forward; data
extraction with source locations; risk-of-bias appraisal; and only then synthesis, if the extracted
evidence supports it.
