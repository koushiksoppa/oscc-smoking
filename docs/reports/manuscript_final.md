# Smoked tobacco use and the risk of squamous cell carcinoma of the oral cavity: a systematic review protocol and interim report of study selection

---

**Manuscript status.** This is a protocol and interim study-selection report, not a results-bearing
systematic review. No study has yet been assessed for eligibility at full text, no datum has been
extracted, no risk of bias appraised and no estimate pooled. Sections 3.3 to 3.9, and the parts of
Section 4 that depend on them, are reported as *not yet available* with the conditions for their
completion stated. No result appears anywhere in this manuscript that the underlying data do not
support.

---

## Abstract

**Background.** Smoked tobacco is an established carcinogen of the upper aerodigestive tract, yet
estimates of its association with squamous cell carcinoma arising specifically in the oral cavity
remain dispersed across a literature that frequently reports outcomes pooled across head-and-neck
subsites, restricts the exposure to cigarettes, and adjusts inconsistently for alcohol, smokeless
tobacco and betel quid.

**Objective.** To determine whether smoked tobacco use is associated with an increased risk of
squamous cell carcinoma of the oral cavity.

**Methods.** A protocol was specified in advance. The outcome is oral cavity cancer with squamous
cell carcinoma histologically confirmed or a stated majority of at least 90% of cases; estimates
pooled across head-and-neck subsites are ineligible unless an oral-cavity-specific estimate is
reported. The exposure is any combusted tobacco product; smokeless tobacco and betel quid are
treated as confounders, not exposures. Eligible designs are case–control and cohort studies
reporting an odds ratio, risk ratio or hazard ratio with a 95% confidence interval, or sufficient
raw data to compute one. One database, PubMed, has been searched to date. Screening is performed by
a single reviewer against a controlled vocabulary of exclusion reasons, and every decision records
the basis on which it was made.

**Results.** The executed search retrieved 194 records, which resolved to 194 unique reports after
deduplication, with no duplicates and no record pairs requiring adjudication. A provisional pass
classified 109 reports for exclusion and 85 for retrieval. These classifications were generated by
an automated procedure and adopted without individual human adjudication; under the review's own
protocol they do not constitute completed screening, and the formal flow therefore reports 0 reports
screened and 0 taken forward. No full text has been sought, retrieved or assessed. No study has been
included, no datum extracted, no risk of bias appraised and no estimate pooled.

**Conclusion.** *Not yet available.* No conclusion about the association between smoked tobacco and
oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma can be drawn from this work in its present state. The search as
executed covers one database with a query narrower than the protocol's own eligibility criteria,
and this is the principal threat to the validity of any future synthesis built upon it.

**Registration.** Not registered. Prospective registration is an open decision, and the provisional
screening pass preceded it; this is reported as a protocol deviation.

**Keywords:** mouth neoplasms; carcinoma, squamous cell; smoking; tobacco products; case–control
studies; cohort studies; systematic review; meta-analysis; PRISMA.

## 1. Introduction

Cancer of the oral cavity is among the more common malignancies worldwide and its incidence is
markedly uneven geographically, with a disproportionate burden in South and Southeast Asia. The
great majority of oral cavity malignancies are squamous cell carcinomas arising from the mucosal
lining. Survival is strongly stage-dependent and a substantial proportion of cases present late, so
primary prevention, and therefore accurate characterisation of modifiable risk factors, carries
weight out of proportion to the advances available in treatment.

Tobacco smoking is an established carcinogen for the upper aerodigestive tract. Quantifying its
association with oral cavity cancer specifically is nevertheless complicated by three recurring
features of the primary literature.

First, many studies report outcomes pooled across head-and-neck subsites, combining oral cavity,
oropharyngeal, hypopharyngeal and laryngeal disease whose aetiologies differ. The contribution of
human papillomavirus to oropharyngeal carcinoma in particular means that a pooled head-and-neck
estimate cannot be read as an oral cavity estimate.

Second, the exposure is frequently reduced to cigarette smoking. Bidis, cigars, pipes, cheroots and
other combusted products dominate exposure in several of the highest-incidence populations, and a
cigarette-only review would systematically omit the literature most informative about them.

Third, alcohol consumption, smokeless tobacco and betel quid are strongly correlated with smoking in
many settings and are variably adjusted for, so published estimates are not directly comparable
across studies without recording the adjustment set for each.

A review restricted to oral-cavity-specific outcomes, admitting the full range of combusted tobacco
products, and recording confounder adjustment explicitly for every estimate, is therefore warranted.

## 2. Methods

This review is designed and reported in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 statement [1], with search
reporting following the PRISMA-S extension [2] and the conduct of a review of observational studies
following the MOOSE recommendations [3]. Methodological conventions not otherwise attributed follow
the Cochrane Handbook [4].

### 2.1 Review question and eligibility criteria

**Review question.** Is smoked tobacco use associated with an increased risk of squamous cell
carcinoma of the oral cavity? The question is framed as *smoked tobacco*, not cigarettes alone. Any
association identified will be reported as an association; no causal claim will be made from
observational data.

**Table 1. Eligibility criteria.**

| Element | Criterion | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Population | Human adults; no restriction by country, sex or setting | Settled |
| Exposure | Any smoked tobacco: cigarettes, bidis, cigars, pipes and other combusted products | Settled |
| Comparator | Never-smokers, and/or "non-smokers" as defined by the study; recorded verbatim in all cases | **Open** |
| Outcome | Oral cavity cancer, SCC histologically confirmed or a stated majority of at least 90% | Settled |
| Contrast | Primary exposure contrast (ever / current / former versus reference) | **Open** |
| Designs | Case–control; cohort, prospective or retrospective | Settled |
| Effect data | OR, RR or HR with a 95% CI, or sufficient raw data to compute one | Settled |
| Language | Published in English | Settled |

Estimates pooled across head-and-neck subsites are **ineligible** unless an oral-cavity-specific
estimate is reported separately. Smokeless tobacco and betel quid are **confounders, not
exposures**, and adjustment for each is recorded per estimate.

Exclusion is recorded against a fixed controlled vocabulary; free text may not substitute for a
code. Codes available at title and abstract are `wrong_population`, `wrong_outcome`,
`wrong_exposure`, `wrong_design`, `review_or_meta`, `conference_abstract`, `animal_or_invitro`,
`not_english` and `other`, the last requiring written justification. Four further codes —
`no_reference_group`, `no_usable_data`, `outcome_not_separable` and `duplicate_publication` — are
restricted to full text, on the grounds that they cannot be judged from an abstract without
guessing. The stage assignment of these codes remains a proposal awaiting endorsement.

**Criteria not yet settled.** Ten questions of anatomical and histological scope remain open: the
eligible subsite list; the treatment of soft palate and uvula, where staging and anatomical
conventions genuinely conflict; the handling of straddling topography codes; a site-purity threshold
analogous to the histological threshold; whether squamous cell carcinoma variants count as squamous
cell carcinoma; the status of adenosquamous and sarcomatoid entities; and whether populations
restricted to premalignant oral lesions are in scope, which affects 14 of the 85 reports
provisionally carried forward. Two of these require a reference text that was not accessible during
protocol development. All must be resolved before full-text eligibility assessment begins.

Query string, identical for v1 and v1r:

```
("Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma"[Title/Abstract] OR OSCC[Title/Abstract])
AND (smoking[Title/Abstract] OR cigarette[Title/Abstract])
AND ("case-control"[Title/Abstract] OR cohort[Title/Abstract])
```

**Deviation from the v1 strategy, reported as such.** The re-execution was run without the Humans
and English database filters. The Humans filter operates on an index term absent from records not
yet indexed — 42 of the 194 here, which are the most recent and plausibly among the most relevant.
Non-English and non-human records are instead excluded at screening with a recorded reason code,
which is auditable in a way a database filter is not. The consequence, which must be reported, is
that `not_english` and `wrong_population` appear as exclusion reasons that a filtered search would
never have surfaced.

**Search dates.** The execution date of the v1r search is **not recorded**; index dates within the
export bound it to on or after 25 October 2026. The execution date of the original v1 search is
**unrecoverable** from any surviving file. Both are reported as unverified rather than estimated.

**Reconciliation.** On 29 October 2026 the v1 query string was submitted to PubMed as a
**counts-only interrogation for strategy validation**: the number of matching records was read back
and nothing was retrieved, exported or screened. It returned 194 records unfiltered and 150 with the
Humans and English filters applied, against the 138 recorded in the v1 strategy document. Three
records had entered the database since the project began. A residual discrepancy of approximately
nine records remains **unexplained and is recorded as an open question**. The documented count has
not been altered to remove the discrepancy.

**Status of the counts-only interrogations.** Two such interrogations are reported in this
manuscript — the v1 reconciliation and the v2 draft counts of 29 October 2026, and the corrected
strategy's counts of 12 November 2026, given in the supplementary material. Both were counts-only
queries run to validate query syntax and estimate retrieval volume. **Neither was a formal
literature search. Neither produced records for screening, an export, a checksum or a registry
entry**, and no record reported in this review derives from either. The only search contributing
records is the v1r execution registered as `pubmed_v1r`. Counts of this kind describe a database on
the day they are taken and change as it grows.

**Limitations of the executed query.** The v1 string is substantially narrower than the eligibility
criteria it is meant to serve. It requires the literal phrase "Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma" or
"OSCC" in the title or abstract; 68 of the 194 retrieved records use the phrase "oral cancer", the
dominant phrasing in aetiological epidemiology, and were retrieved only because they also happened
to contain one of the required terms. It reduces the exposure to "smoking" or "cigarette", omitting
bidi, cigar, pipe and other product terms central to the exposure definition, and it uses no
controlled vocabulary at all. Half the retrieved set — 98 of 194 — matched the design block only
through the single word "cohort", which also captures prognostic studies of patients who already
have the disease. **Any synthesis based on this record set alone would rest on a search that does
not match its own protocol.**

A corrected and substantially broader strategy has been prepared and its syntax validated by a
counts-only interrogation, but it has **not been executed**; it is reproduced in the supplementary
material. It supersedes the earlier v2 draft, from which it differs by the removal of a
site-unrestricted histology term that had allowed squamous cell carcinomas of other anatomical sites
into the outcome block. Its retrieval volume and database set are open decisions.

### 2.4 Study selection

Screening is performed by **one reviewer**. This is a resource constraint, declared openly, and is
reported as a limitation under PRISMA 2020 items 8 and 9 [1]. There is no second screener and no
duplicate screening at any stage.

Records are deduplicated before screening by persistent identifier and by an exact
author–year–title fingerprint. Records whose titles are merely similar are flagged for human review
and never merged automatically, because deciding that two records describe the same report is a
judgement.

**Automated classification and its status.** An automated procedure generated a suggested decision
and, where applicable, a suggested exclusion reason for each record from its title and abstract.
That suggestion set is retained as an audit artefact. Each worksheet row records both the suggestion
and the basis on which the recorded decision was made, using two values: `adopted_from_suggestion`,
meaning the suggestion was accepted without the record being judged individually; and
`independently_adjudicated`, meaning the reviewer judged the record from its own evidence.

**An automated suggestion cannot satisfy a decision field.** A stage containing any adopted row is
not a screened stage, however complete it appears. The formal PRISMA flow counts independently
adjudicated decisions only, and adopted classifications are reported separately as a provisional
outcome. A decision counts as independently adjudicated only when all five of the following are
recorded: a decision, a reviewer, a screening date, the basis `independently_adjudicated`, and a
separate adjudication date.

**Blinded verification.** Because the recorded classifications are identical to the automated
suggestions, checking them against the same source would be circular. A blinded verification sample
of 40 of the 194 records (20.6%) was drawn by simple random sampling without replacement, with the
recorded decision withheld, using a seed derived from the data rather than chosen. The sample size,
acceptance thresholds and a one-directional sweep rule were all fixed before the sample was drawn
and before any response existed. Agreement is computed at the record level on the decision value
alone; Cohen's kappa [6] is reported as a descriptive statistic and is not an acceptance criterion.
This exercise compares **one reviewer's blinded decisions against an automated suggestion set**. It
is not inter-reviewer agreement and not independent duplicate screening, and a favourable result
would not retrospectively convert adopted records into adjudicated ones. The methodology is
specified and implemented but **has not yet been formally adopted as a protocol decision**.

### 2.5 Data extraction

Extraction is performed by one reviewer into a fixed schema of 34 study-level and 47 estimate-level
fields. Three principles are enforced by validation rather than left to discipline: reported and
calculated values never share a field; missing is a value, recorded explicitly as *not reported*,
*not applicable* or *unclear*, and never as blank, zero or inferred; and every extracted number
carries a source location naming the page, table or figure it came from.

Recorded per study: design, setting, centres, recruitment period, participant and case–control
counts, age and sex distribution, histological confirmation and reported percentage of squamous cell
carcinoma, anatomical subsites verbatim, whether an oral-cavity-specific estimate is separable, case
definition, the matching variables used in design, and the method by which exposure was ascertained.

Recorded per estimate: the effect measure and its confidence interval as reported; the covariate
list verbatim; separate indicator fields for adjustment for alcohol, age, sex, socioeconomic status,
smokeless tobacco, betel quid and human papillomavirus; exposure and comparator definitions
verbatim; product types; and smoking intensity, duration, pack-years and any other cumulative metric,
each with its own value and unit field so that a study reporting several is not reduced to one.
Crude and adjusted estimates are held separately, and the derivation of any calculated value is
recorded with its inputs.

Two fields carry an explicit unresolved status in the schema and cannot be treated as
protocol-conformant until the corresponding questions are settled: the comparator type and the
exposure contrast.

**No data have been extracted.** The extraction dataset does not exist.

### 2.6 Risk-of-bias assessment

**No instrument has been selected and no assessment has been performed.**

The appraisal table is specified instrument-agnostically — one row per study per domain, recording
the instrument and its version, the domain, the judgement on the instrument's own scale, supporting
evidence quoted or located in the paper, the reviewer's justification, whether information was
missing, and who assessed it. Domain names will come from the chosen instrument and are not
invented. No numeric summary score will be computed unless the chosen instrument explicitly defines
one, and missing information will not be treated as evidence of high risk of bias.

Three candidate instruments have been set out for decision: the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale [7], which
has design-specific forms for case–control and cohort studies and yields a study-level total but
collapses distinct domains into an unweighted score; ROBINS-E [8], purpose-built for exposure
epidemiology, which handles confounding explicitly and requires a pre-specified confounder list at
the cost of substantially more work per study; and Newcastle–Ottawa reported per domain without a
total, which avoids the composite-score objection but is a non-standard use of a standard instrument
and removes the study-level summary that subgroup and sensitivity analyses would otherwise use. The
choice has a second component that cannot be deferred: which confounders a study must address for
the confounding or comparability domain to be judged adequate — alcohol alone, or alcohol together
with smokeless tobacco and betel quid. An instrument that asks whether confounding was handled
adequately is unanswerable until "adequately" is defined, and defining it after seeing the studies
would be post hoc. **This decision is pending.**

### 2.7 Effect measures

Standard errors are derived as `SE = (ln(upper) − ln(lower)) / (2 × 1.96)` where a 95% confidence
interval is reported, and from raw cell counts where only a 2 × 2 table is given; which route was
used is recorded per estimate, and reported and calculated values are held in separate fields. At
most one estimate per study enters any single analysis. Fundamentally different effect measures are
not combined without a documented justification, and no transformation is applied merely to make
pooling easier. For a rare outcome such as oral cavity cancer the odds ratio approximates the risk
ratio, but that argument will be made explicitly with reference to observed baseline risks rather
than assumed.

### 2.8 Synthesis methods

The analysis implementation exists and is tested; it has not been run, and it refuses to run,
because no study has been included and no estimate extracted.

Specified in advance: heterogeneity quantified by Cochran's Q and I², with τ² reported for
random-effects models; a random-effects model is anticipated, with the DerSimonian–Laird [5] and
restricted maximum likelihood estimators both implemented. Planned subgroup analyses, contingent on
sufficient studies, are product type (cigarette-only versus mixed), geographical region, anatomical
subsite, risk-of-bias level, and alcohol-adjusted versus unadjusted estimates. Planned sensitivity
analyses include leave-one-out influence analysis and restriction by risk of bias.

Not yet specified, because each depends on evidence that does not exist: the primary effect measure;
whether case–control and cohort estimates may be pooled at all or must be analysed separately; the
model estimator and whether a small-sample adjustment is applied, which depends on the number of
studies; the exposure contrast and reference-group handling, both open protocol questions; and the
rule for choosing among several adjusted models reported in one paper, which is recommended but not
adopted.

**Criteria for not pooling** are specified in advance: a narrative synthesis is the stated
alternative where pooling is not defensible, and pooling will not proceed across different
anatomical sites without a valid oral-specific estimate, across smokeless and smoked tobacco, across
incompatible exposure contrasts, or across duplicate or overlapping estimates treated as independent
studies.

**Declared implementation limitations.** The restricted maximum likelihood estimator has no external
validation in this implementation; it is tested for self-consistency and limiting behaviour only,
and any estimate produced with it must be cross-checked against an established implementation before
publication. No Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman interval is implemented, which the Cochrane Handbook [4]
favours when the number of studies is small and between-study variance is non-zero. No
Mantel–Haenszel or Peto method is implemented, so rare-event and zero-cell data have no route. No
meta-regression or dose–response model is implemented. The pooled estimate, weights and τ² have no
external reference check.

### 2.9 Reporting standards and software

Reporting follows PRISMA 2020 [1] and PRISMA-S [2]; conduct follows MOOSE [3] and the Cochrane
Handbook [4]. All analysis is implemented in Python 3.14 with pinned dependencies (`scipy` 1.18.1,
`matplotlib` 3.11.2, `pyyaml` 6.0.3, `pytest` 9.1.1). The raw export is immutable and
checksum-verified, derived datasets rebuild byte-for-byte from it, and the full test suite comprises
671 collected tests.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Principal findings

No finding about the association between smoked tobacco use and squamous cell carcinoma of the oral
cavity is available from this work. What can be stated is the position of the review itself: a
protocol with most eligibility criteria settled and several questions of scope still open; one
database searched with a query narrower than the protocol requires; 194 unique reports identified;
and a provisional screening pass that has not been independently adjudicated and therefore
contributes no PRISMA-countable outcome.

### 4.2 Interpretation

The gap between the 194 records retrieved and the several thousand a protocol-conformant strategy
returns is the most consequential feature of the present position. It indicates that the executed
search samples a small and probably unrepresentative fraction of the eligible literature:
specifically, that part of it which happens to use the phrase "oral squamous cell carcinoma" or
"OSCC" together with "smoking" or "cigarette". Studies reporting "oral cancer" with stated
histology, or exposure to bidis or other combusted products, are systematically less likely to be
retrieved. Any estimate derived from this record set would therefore be at material risk of
selection bias of unknown direction, and the decision on search scope should be taken before, rather
than after, screening effort is invested.

The distinction maintained throughout between a provisional classification and an adjudicated
decision is not a formality. All 194 recorded classifications are byte-identical to the automated
suggestions, with no per-record human approval. Reporting them as completed screening would misstate
what has been done, and would also misstate what the blinded verification exercise can establish: it
compares one reviewer's blinded decisions against an automated suggestion set, and even a high
agreement rate would not make unadjudicated records adjudicated.

### 4.3 Comparison with previous evidence

**Deferred.** A comparison with previously published estimates requires this review's own estimates,
which do not exist. Presenting published figures here without a synthesis to compare them against
would imply agreement or disagreement that has not been established, so no such comparison is
offered.

### 4.4 Biological and epidemiological context

Combusted tobacco products share the carcinogenic mechanisms relevant to oral mucosa, which is the
basis on which the protocol admits bidis, cigars, pipes and other products alongside cigarettes
rather than restricting the exposure to cigarette smoking. Smokeless tobacco and betel quid have
distinct dose–response relationships and are strongly associated with both smoking and oral cancer,
which is the basis on which the protocol treats them as confounders requiring recorded adjustment
rather than as part of the exposure. These are the protocol's stated rationales; this review
provides no new evidence bearing on them.

### 4.5 Strengths

The protocol fixes the outcome and exposure definitions, the exclusion vocabulary and the analysis
conventions in advance, with each traceable to a dated decision record. The raw export is immutable
and checksum-verified, and the derived datasets rebuild byte-for-byte from it. The provenance of
every screening classification is recorded in the data rather than in prose, and the reporting
machinery refuses to count an unadjudicated classification as a screening outcome. The blinded
verification instrument, its sample size, its seed derivation and its acceptance thresholds were all
fixed before any response was recorded.

### 4.6 Limitations

1. **Search coverage.** One database has been searched. PubMed alone does not constitute a
   systematic search, and no decision has been taken on which further sources to include.
2. **Search sensitivity.** The executed query is narrower than the protocol's own eligibility
   criteria. This is the dominant threat to validity at present.
3. **Lost original export.** The v1 export does not survive and has not been recreated. A residual
   discrepancy of about nine records in the reconciliation is unexplained.
4. **Unrecorded search dates.** Neither the v1 nor the v1r execution date is recorded.
5. **Single reviewer.** No duplicate screening, extraction or appraisal at any stage.
6. **Screening not adjudicated.** All 194 classifications rest on an adopted automated suggestion.
7. **Verification incomplete.** The blinded sample is unanswered; no agreement figure exists.
8. **Scope questions open.** Ten questions of anatomical and histological scope remain unresolved.
9. **No registration.** The review is not prospectively registered, and the provisional screening
   pass preceded any registration decision.
10. **Analysis implementation.** The limitations declared in Section 2.8 apply to any future
    synthesis.
11. **No peer review of the search.** No PRESS or equivalent peer review of any search strategy has
    been performed.

### 4.7 Implications

**For practice and policy.** None. No evidence is presented here that could support a
recommendation.

**For research conduct.** The project demonstrates a reporting arrangement in which an automated
classification cannot be mistaken for a human screening decision: the two are distinguished in the
data, enforced at validation, and reported in separate sections of the flow. Where automated
assistance is used in screening, this separation is what allows a reader to see what was actually
adjudicated.

**For completion of this review.** The outstanding work is substantial, is set out in Section 5, and
is human work that cannot be generated.

## 5. Conclusion

**No conclusion regarding the association between smoked tobacco use and squamous cell carcinoma of
the oral cavity can be drawn from this work at present.**

A protocol has been specified and a single database searched, yielding 194 unique reports. A
provisional screening pass exists but has not been independently adjudicated and therefore
contributes nothing to the formal reporting flow. No full text has been retrieved, no study
included, no datum extracted, no risk of bias appraised and no estimate pooled. The search as
executed is narrower than the protocol requires and covers one database.

Completion requires, in order: a decision on search coverage and on the volume implied by the
expanded strategy; independent adjudication of the 194 classifications and completion of the blinded
verification sample; resolution of the open scope, comparator and contrast questions and of the
risk-of-bias instrument; retrieval and eligibility assessment of the reports carried forward; data
extraction with source locations; risk-of-bias appraisal; and only then synthesis, if the extracted
evidence supports it.
